One thing we see over and over again in our stories is the concept of secret birthright. The notion there, expressed variously, is that the hero does not come from where they think they come from. Their parents or other forebears have some powerful mystery that is only disclosed over time, or through accident or necessity. We see it in Harry Potter and Star Wars (and Spaceballs), in the Little Lame Prince and in Rapunzel. There are variations, where the hero knows where they come from but cannot reveal it, as in Bear Skin or the Count of Monte Cristo.

We feel it when we are teenagers. I remember going to the mall with my parents and walking fifty paces in front of them, as if to say "these are not my parents, they were assigned to me by the government. My _real_ parents are spies" or something. There is a feeling that what we see cannot be all we come from.

I love the official phrase: "cosmic, mysterious, ancestry." You are from someplace bigger, someplace that perhaps cannot be fully understood, but that mystery, an aspect of _the_ Mystery, will come to bear in your life and experience when it needs to.

It is a question of birthright. Whose child are you, really, and what does that mean for who you are and what you must do? As somebody who has been called upon to break chains in my own life and upbringing, this means a lot to me. I think it probably means a lot to everyone, which is why it's part of so many of the stories we tell, and tell themselves in us.

So who _aren't_ you? You know you are not just your biology or sociology or geography or religiosity or politics or finances. You are not a zip code or a set of vital statistics. In one of my books I talk about what people talk about at funerals; it's never the numbers and not the data. Self comes from someplace else.

So if that is true, who will you let in? What and who pays into that stream of self? *That* is your family. *Those* are your parents and brothers and sisters, and also your children; your responsibility. This is not DNA. This is destiny. And it is choice.

You are each and every character in the stories you tell. The deep resonating truth about the characters is a deep resonating truth about you, or you would not keep telling the stories and going to these movies. So what does that tell you about who you are? 

The real *you* of you comes from an undiscovered country. The reason bad things feel bad is that they are not part of your makeup, not part of your true lineage, no matter what you have been made to accept. This disconnect is proof of your mysterious cosmic ancestry. 

You are secretly royal. How will you rule?
