The people who say that they want to live in the Good Old Days, go back to a certain set of values, and so on, are talking in part about something that never really happened. It is an imaginary time, as we know, given that you can point back to any given point in history and find all manner of things that were horrible, things that we ought to be glad to have overcome.

For that part, it is a little like being nostalgic about the second world war. It was a truly horrible time, and it almost came out pretty poorly there. But we, having the ability to look back from the point of view of the good guys winning, can see it as a black and white unqualified success. More than that, we _know_ how it ends, and so we can pretend that it was like that for folks going through it.

It is, I think, the reason we watch the same movies over and over again; the tension is already resolved because we are aware of the resolution. My grandson hears the opening sounds of Mary Poppins and has already "seen" the whole thing in his head, to the point where he asks us to fast forward.

But that's not the whole thing. Something else is happening there, too, when people romanticize the past. It is very possible that what they want is a return to a very specific perspective and a very specific story. America might well have felt "great" in the past if you were white, and male, and rich. If that was the only story that could be told, and if it was _your_ story, I suppose I can understand, even though it requires a departure from empathy and, I would argue, humanity.

So maybe what is happening is that as we move forward we are hearing a bunch of different stories from a bunch of different perspectives, and some of those are quite challenging -- challenging to the point where some folks want those alternative narratives repressed, censored, erased, if not now than some time in the past when we did not see those stories and perspectives as valid in the first place.

It is a thief of stories, this nostalgia.

The good news is that we cannot really go back and we cannot really unlearn. Not permanently, not completely, not ever. The other stories come out, the other voices find themselves and find each other and find us. They get louder. Historical arcs bend. We grow. Together.
