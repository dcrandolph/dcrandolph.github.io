The family at Wendy's paused for a public meal blessing. Dad, Mom, Kids 1 and 2, all with careful haircuts and tucked-in shirts and excellent posture, broke the rule about elbows on the table to bow their heads against palms together in a tableau that Norman Rockwell would have thought was a bit much.

I was seventeen, enjoying a Frosty and a side order of fries with my friend Jeff. Jeff's fraternal twin brother Jay was behind the counter in a yellow paper hat. Whenever the manager was away Jay would give us large fries for the price of smalls. Conspiracy. Rebellion. 

And here, orthodoxy.

The Dad's prayer went on for a bit, longer than it needed to, louder than it needed to for the purposes of the people at their table and, really, louder and longer than it needed to for the purposes of everyone else's, there by the baked potato bar and the paper ketchup cups and the garbage cans that Jay really should have emptied by now.

Who was this for? Them, us, God? Who was hard of hearing and who needed to be reminded? 

Are you there? Is this thing on?

None of the rest of us in what I will generously call a restaurant knew what to say or where to look, so we all just waited. I felt a sense of thanksgiving that I had a spoon instead of a straw for my Frosty, lest the slurping noise interrupt the devotional.

I was really grateful that nobody said anything. That would have been even worse than this weird performance art piece unfolding before us.

Should I have said something?

Probably. I grew up in a church where we didn't pray _that_ kind of prayers. We weren't _that_ kind of people, though most folks who showed up used to be. Like most converts from anything to anything, lots of time and effort is spent drawing caricatures of the from.

We spent a lot of Sundays congratulating ourselves on our openness, enlightenment, egalitarianism, and respect. We also spent a lot of Sundays making fun of people who were not as evolved as we were. For us, brilliant us, God could never be a big guy in the sky someplace, maybe on a throne, maybe bearded, definitely irritated. Absentee landlord at best. At worst? Watch out. 

Wait 'til your Father comes home.

Those second-person prayers, the gospel of Saint Wendy, were just not done. Beneath us. We were nonjudgemental in our harsh judgement of the "old" ways, of anything conversational, second-person. You and me: "God, please give me the winning lottery numbers." "Lord, please smite mine enemies in today's HR meeting." "Father, please look the other way, just this once, and I promise I will be good after this." "Heavenly Dad, please grant me the serenity to deal with your abuse." 

Our prayers were not second person. Our prayers were not _at_ but _about._ We _affirmed_ things:

> The Light of God surrounds us,
> The Love of God enfolds us,
> The Power of God protects us,
> The Presence of God watches over us.
> Wherever we are, God is,
> And all is well.

That was a long time ago, but that is one of my favorites, still. I say it all the time, still. But now, after all these years, I wonder if we've lost something. Where is God, if you are talking directly to Him in a fast food restaurant? But where is God, if you are talking about Him like He's not there, like a family deciding what to do with an aging and heard-of-hearing great-grandpa in a nursing home when nobody wants to be responsible for care? Same place.

"What do you wanna do with Pop?"

Amen. 

I get it. If I make God into a big version of myself, complete with all my same likes, dislikes, pet peeves, pet people, it's just ego. But, really, how is it NOT ego if what I am doing is affirming something that I can not or do not address directly?

At best, I am submitting a to-do list or a lunch order. It's all ego and command, which is why it only ever works a little bit. At worst, I realize that it's just me, talking to myself. Organized wishful thinking. More ego. 

`The Divine has left the chat.`

If the Light surrounds and the Love enfolds and all the rest, if I am thinking and talking about something that is not just here but _here_ in the way that a wave might talk about the ocean, maybe I need to rethink this.

I am, you are, we are, in the room with all there is to Love, the source of all Life, the beauty that created and creates. Should we not address It, and in so doing open out and up and grow? Or will we just keep talking to ourselves, in cold lonely ineffectual self-congratulation?
