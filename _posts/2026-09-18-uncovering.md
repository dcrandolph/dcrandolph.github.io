It's a long flight from Tampa to Denver. A long way to go yet, and I have already read what I brought with me to read. There is nothing in SkyMall that appeals. Looking forward to complimentary ginger ale and whatever they serve now that they cannot serve peanuts.

Wondering when I will stop noticing the drone of the engines, the squeeze of the seat, the inability to straighten my legs on this first leg of a journey that ends in California for a work trip where I do not want to do the work and where I do not want to go to a mandatory Team Building and where I do not want to do anything, really, but come back home. What exactly is that smell? No frequent flyer miles are worth this.

I suspect the toddler in the row in front of me, across the aisle, is thinking all the same things. Nobody likes Team Building. But the kid seems to like me. I get a smile, the first one I have seen since before the security checkpoint this morning.

We decide to play peek-a-boo. Charades are out of the question, and Rochambeau is too much for somebody with developing fine motor skills.

There is no natural end to this game. It will only be cut short a half hour later when the beverage cart breaks our line of sight and mom switches shoulders. In the meantime, though, the novelty does not, can not, wear out. The discovery never gets old.

Kids do that though, don't they? They move in an act of uncovering and removing, which from a material level seems backwards. They don't _have_ anything, so what are they getting rid of and unearthing? But of course that is what a grownup would say, isn't it? I have lots of stuff, and lots to lose, or I wouldn't be on this trip.

At some point the action changes, the tide comes in. Adulthood tends to be marked by covering up, with ego, with baggage, with opinions. Is this why we get old, or is this what getting old is?

In any case, I am sick of it. I feel buried alive. Time to remember old games and make them new.

So many people want to find faith, or to love themselves, or to get an answer. If there was no faith, what would cause them to think they could find it? If there was no love, why try? If there were no answers, why ask? The answer is in the question, and the love is in you. That is faith. We can wait, or we can play. Something is hiding right behind our hands.

The discovery never gets old.
