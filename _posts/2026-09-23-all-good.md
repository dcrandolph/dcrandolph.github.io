Sadness is acceptable; it's okay. It's just not _compulsory._ How many times, during a funeral or something similar (even if it just feels like a funeral) are we reminded that so-and-so would have wanted us to be happy?

Sometimes we feel sad, and there is no reason to judge that. I am talking about sadness as a commodity; the idea that we are _supposed_ to feel sad in order to fit in to the social situation, that it is appropriate, and therefore mandatory, when there is so much beauty in any situation.

**Shall we call everything good? Yes.**

If worry does not contribute to a positive outcome, does fear? Does anger, frustration, sadness? Only in the abstract, and in every case we find that it would not have been necessary except that we'd painted ourselves into a corner of one kind or another.

What if the appropriate response is always delight, and that any other response is fine but less efficacious? 

I have decided to be unwelcome in places where sadness is compulsory.

I want to laugh in the face of danger. I want to be joyous and brave the way I know, I know I was meant to be.

I want to be serious like a child at play.
